# Prudential Regulation Authority - Supervisory Statement SS1/21: Operational Resilience

**Jurisdiction:** United Kingdom (Bank of England / PRA)  
**Document ID:** DOC-PRA-SS121-2021  
**Classification:** Public Regulation  
**Effective Date:** 2022-03-31 (Full Implementation 2025)  
**Permitted Roles:** ["analyst", "manager", "auditor"]  

---

## Chapter 2: Important Business Services (IBS)

### Clause PRA-SS121-2.1 (Identification of Important Business Services)
Firms must identify and maintain an inventory of their important business services. An important business service is defined as a service provided by a firm to external clients where a prolonged disruption could cause intolerable harm to consumers or pose systemic risk to the stability of the UK financial system.

### Clause PRA-SS121-2.4 (Annual Review of Services Inventory)
Firms must review their important business services at least annually, or promptly following any material change to business operations, internal infrastructure, legal entity structure, or acquisition of new business divisions.

## Chapter 3: Impact Tolerances and Severe Stress Testing

### Clause PRA-SS121-3.1 (Setting Impact Tolerances)
For each important business service, a firm must set an impact tolerance. The impact tolerance must specify the maximum tolerable level of disruption, expressed as a metric of time (e.g., maximum outage duration in hours or days) and qualitative metrics, beyond which intolerable harm to customers or markets occurs.

### Clause PRA-SS121-3.3 (Severe but Plausible Scenario Testing)
Firms must test their ability to remain within impact tolerances under severe but plausible operational disruption scenarios. Scenarios must include simultaneous failures across key systems, widespread cyberattacks, infrastructure outages, and third-party supplier insolvencies.

---

## Chapter 4: Mapping and Lessons Learned

### Clause PRA-SS121-4.2 (Operational Interdependency Mapping)
Firms must map the resources, personnel, technology architecture, facilities, and third-party dependencies required to deliver each important business service. Mapping documentation must be sufficiently granular to identify single points of failure and operational vulnerabilities.
